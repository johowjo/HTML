Let $\vb{x}$ be from some probability distribution $P(\vb{x})$, that is
\[
  \vb{x} \sim P(\vb{x})
\]
Also, let the target function be $f(\vb{x})$
Then we have
\begin{align*}
  E_\text{out}(G) &= \underset{x\sim P}{\mathbb{E}}[\llbracket f(\vb{x}) \neq G(\vb{x})\rrbracket] \\ 
  &= \mathbb{P}[f(\vb{x})\neq G(\vb{x})]
\end{align*}
Observe that for some $\vb{x}$, if there are more than $M + 1$ different $t$ such that $g_t(\vb{x}) = 1$, then
\begin{align*}
  G(\vb{x}) &= \text{sign}\left(\sum^{2M + 1}_{t = 1}g_t(\vb{x})\right) \\ 
  &= \text{sign}\left(\overbrace{1 + 1 +\dots + 1}^{\geq 2M + 1}\overbrace{-1 - 1 -\dots - 1}^{\leq 2M}\right) = 1
\end{align*}
and $G(\vb{x}) = 1$ \\ 
Similarly, for some $\vb{x}$, if there are more than $M + 1$ different $t$ such that $g_t(\vb{x}) = -1$, then $G(\vb{x}) = -1$. \\ 
From this observation, we can conclude that for some $(y_n, \vb{x}_n)\in\mathcal{D}$, $G$ wrongly classifies it if and only if there are more than $M + 1$ different $t$ such that $g_t$ wrongly classifies $(y_n, \vb{x}_n)$. \\ 
Hence 
\[
  f(\vb{x}) \neq G(\vb{x}_n) \Longleftrightarrow \sum^{2M + 1}_{t = 1}\llbracket f(\vb{x})\neq g_t(\vb{w})\rrbracket \geq M + 1
\]
Therefore
\begin{align*}
  E_\text{out}(G) &= \mathbb{P}[f(\vb{x})\neq G(\vb{x})] \\ 
  &= \mathbb{P}[\sum^{2M + 1}_{t = 1}\llbracket f(\vb{x})\neq g_t(\vb{x})\rrbracket \geq M + 1] \\ 
  &= \sum^{2M + 1}_{m = M + 1}\left(\mathbb{P}[\sum^{2M + 1}_{t = 1}\llbracket f(\vb{x})\neq g_t(\vb{x})\rrbracket = m]\right) \\
  &\leq \frac{1}{M + 1}\sum^{2M + 1}_{m = M + 1}\left(m\cdot\mathbb{P}[\sum^{2M + 1}_{t = 1}\llbracket f(\vb{x})\neq g_t(\vb{x})\rrbracket = m]\right) \\
  &\leq \frac{1}{M + 1}\sum^{2M + 1}_{m = 1}\left(m\cdot\mathbb{P}[\sum^{2M + 1}_{t = 1}\llbracket f(\vb{x})\neq g_t(\vb{x})\rrbracket = m]\right) \\
  &= \frac{1}{M + 1}\underset{x\sim P}{\mathbb{E}}[\sum^{2M + 1}_{t = 1}\llbracket f(\vb{x})\neq g_t(\vb{x})\rrbracket]\\
  &= \frac{1}{M + 1}\sum^{2M + 1}_{t = 1}\underset{x\sim P}{\mathbb{E}}[\llbracket f(\vb{x})\neq g_t(\vb{x})\rrbracket] \\ 
  &= \frac{1}{M + 1}\sum^{2M + 1}_{t = 1}E_\text{out}(g_t) \\ 
  &= \frac{1}{M + 1}\sum^{2M + 1}_{t = 1}e_t
\end{align*}
So $\frac{1}{M + 1}\sum^{2M + 1}_{t = 1}e_t$ is an upper bound for $E_\text{out}(G)$ \\ 
We then prove that this bound is tight by constructing a case when $E_\text{out}(G)$ meets this bound. \\ 
\par 
Consider the case where $M = 1$, $\vb{x}$ is one-dimensional, and
\begin{align*}
  P(x) &= U[0, 1]\quad(\text{$U$ is the uniform distribution}) \\ 
  f(x) &= 1 \\ 
  g_1(x) &= 
  \begin{cases}
    +1 \quad x \leq \frac{1}{3} \\
    -1 \quad \text{else} 
  \end{cases} \\ 
  g_2(x) &= 
  \begin{cases}
    +1 \quad \frac{1}{3} < x \leq \frac{2}{3} \\
    -1 \quad \text{else} 
  \end{cases} \\
  g_3(x) &= 
  \begin{cases}
    +1 \quad \frac{2}{3} < x \\ 
    -1 \quad \text{else} 
  \end{cases}
\end{align*}
\newpage
In this case, we can see that $e_1 = e_2 = e_3 = \frac{2}{3}$, and $E_\text{out}(G) = 1$ (at least two of $g_1, g_2, g_3$ are wrong). \\ 
So 
\[
  \frac{1}{M + 1}\sum^{2M + 1}_{t = 1}e_t = \frac{\frac{2}{3}\cdot 3}{2} = 1 = E_\text{out}(G)
\]
and the bound is met. \\ 
Therefore, we have proven that this is the tightest bound. \\ 
Q.E.D.

We know that in iteration $t$, $\vb{u}$ is updated in the following manner 
\[
  u_n^{(t + 1)} \leftarrow
  \begin{cases}
    u_n^{(t)}\cdot\sqrt{\frac{1 - \epsilon_t}{\epsilon_t}} & \llbracket y_n\neq g_t(\vb{x}_n)\rrbracket \\
    u_n^{(t)}\cdot\sqrt{\frac{\epsilon_t}{1 - \epsilon_t}} & \llbracket y_n = g_t(\vb{x}_n)\rrbracket \\
  \end{cases}
\]
Therefore
\begin{align}
  U_{t + 1} &= \sum^N_{n = 1}u_n^{(t + 1)} \\ 
  &= \sqrt{\frac{1 - \epsilon_t}{\epsilon_t}}\left(\sum^N_{n = 1}\llbracket y_n\neq g_t(\vb{x}_n)\rrbracket u_n^{(t + 1)}\right) + \sqrt{\frac{\epsilon_t}{1 - \epsilon_t}}\left(\sum^N_{n = 1}\llbracket y_n = g_t(\vb{x}_n)\rrbracket u_n^{(t + 1)}\right) \\ 
  &= \sqrt{\frac{1 - \epsilon_t}{\epsilon_t}}\left(\sum^N_{n = 1}\llbracket y_n\neq g_t(\vb{x}_n)\rrbracket u_n^{(t + 1)}\right) + \sqrt{\frac{\epsilon_t}{1 - \epsilon_t}}\left(\sum^N_{n = 1}(1 - \llbracket y_n \neq g_t(\vb{x}_n)\rrbracket) u_n^{(t + 1)}\right) 
\end{align}
Now consider the definition of $\epsilon_t$ 
\begin{align*}
  \epsilon_t &= \frac{\sum^N_{n = 1}\llbracket y_n\neq g_t(\vb{x}_n)\rrbracket u_n^{(t)}}{\sum^N_{n = 1}u_n^{(t)}} \\ 
  \implies & \sum^N_{n = 1}\llbracket y_n\neq g_t(\vb{x}_n)\rrbracket u_n^{(t)} = \epsilon_t\sum^N_{n = 1}u_n^{(t)}
\end{align*}
And 
\begin{align*}
  1 - \epsilon_t &= 1 - \frac{\sum^N_{n = 1}\llbracket y_n\neq g_t(\vb{x}_n)\rrbracket u_n^{(t)}}{\sum^N_{n = 1}u_n^{(t)}} \\ 
  &= \frac{\sum^N_{n = 1}(1 - \llbracket y_n \neq g_t(\vb{x}_n)\rrbracket) u_n^{(t + 1)}}{\sum^N_{n = 1}u_n^{(t)}} \\
  \implies & \sum^N_{n = 1}(1 - \llbracket y_n \neq g_t(\vb{x}_n)\rrbracket) u_n^{(t + 1)} = (1 - \epsilon_t)\sum^N_{n = 1}u_n^{(t)}
\end{align*}
Substituting into $(3)$ we obtain
\begin{align*}
  U_{t + 1} &= \sqrt{\frac{1 - \epsilon_t}{\epsilon_t}}\epsilon_t\sum^N_{n = 1}u_n^{(t)} + \sqrt{\frac{\epsilon_t}{1 - \epsilon_t}}(1 - \epsilon_t)\sum^N_{n = 1}u_n^{(t)} \\ 
  &= \left(2\sqrt{\epsilon_t(1 - \epsilon_t)}\right)\sum^N_{n = 1}u_n^{(t)} \\ 
  &= 2\sqrt{\epsilon_t(1 - \epsilon_t)}U_t
\end{align*}
Therefore 
\[
  \frac{U_{t + 1}}{U_t} = 2\sqrt{\epsilon_t(1 - \epsilon_t)}
\]
Q.E.D.

% Starting from $s_1, s_2,\dots,s_N = 0$, at $t = 1$, the probem is

% First we find the best $h$ with linear regression. \\ 
% In this case, the error function is the square error
% \[
%   \text{err}(h(\vb{x}_n), y_n) = (h(\vb{x}_n) - y_n)^2

% The hypothesis that minimizes $\sum^N_{n = 1}(h(\vb{x}_n), y_n)$
The first part of the gradient boost decision tree is to find the hypothesis
\[
  \arg\min_h\frac{\eta}{N}\sum^N_{n = 1}\left(h(\vb{x}_n) - (y_n - s_n)\right)^2
\]
Neglecting the constant terms, and utilizing the fact that $s_1 = s_2 =\dots=s_N = 0$ at the beginning of the first iteration, the problem becomes 
\[
  \arg\min_h\sum^N_{n = 1}\left(h(\vb{x}_n) - y_n\right)^2
\]
Generally, the optimal hypothesis $h$ that minimizes the error function above can be found with any regression algorithm, in this problem, our algorithm of choice is linear regression.\\ 
Hence, we have
\[
  h(\vb{x}_n) = \vb{w}^T\vb{x}^\prime
\]
Where
\begin{align*}
  \vb{w} &= [w_0\ w_1\ \dots\ w_N]^T \\ 
  \vb{x}^\prime &= [1\ \vb{w}]^T
\end{align*}
Therefore, the minimization problem becomes
\[
  \min_{\vb{w}}\sum^N_{n = 1}(\vb{w}^T\vb{x}_n^\prime - y_n)^2
\]
Let $g_1(\vb{x})$ be the optimal hypothesis obtained by linear regression, at optimal, we have
\begin{align*}
  & \pdv{}{w_i}\left(\sum^N_{n = 1}(\vb{w}^T\vb{x}_n^\prime - y_n)^2\right) = 0\quad i = 0, 1,\dots,d \\ 
  \implies & \sum^N_{n = 1}(g_1(\vb{x}_n) - y_n){x_n}_i = 0\quad i = 0, 1,\dots,d\ \ (\text{define ${x_n}_0 = 1$})
\end{align*}
\par 
Next, we procede on the next step of the gradient boost decision tree, finding $\alpha_1$
\begin{align*}
  \alpha_1 &= \arg\min_\eta\frac{1}{N}\sum^N_{n = 1}((y_n - s_n) - \eta g_1(\vb{x}_n))^2 \\ 
  &= \arg\min_\eta\sum^N_{n = 1}(y_n - \eta g_1(\vb{x}_n))^2 \\ 
\end{align*}
At optimal we have 
\[
  \pdv{}{\eta}\sum^N_{n = 1}(y_n - \eta g_1(\vb{x}_n))^2 = \sum^N_{n = 1}-g_x(\vb{x}_n)(y_n - \eta g_1(\vb{x}_n)) =  0 
\]
Hence
\begin{align*}
  &\sum^N_{n = 1}g_1(\vb{x}_n)(y_n - \alpha_1g_1(\vb{x}_n)) = 0 \\ 
  \implies & \alpha_1\sum^N_{n = 1}g_1(\vb{x}_n)^2 - \sum^N_{n = 1}g_1(\vb{x}_n)y_n = 0 \\ 
  \implies & \alpha_1 = \frac{\sum^N_{n = 1}g_1(\vb{x}_n)^2}{\sum^N_{n = 1}g_1(\vb{x}_n)y_n}
\end{align*}
\par
Recall the result obtained earlier 
\begin{align*}
  \begin{cases}
    \sum^N_{n = 1}(g_1(\vb{x}_n) - y_n){x_n}_0 = 0 \\
    \sum^N_{n = 1}(g_1(\vb{x}_n) - y_n){x_n}_1 = 0 \\
    \vdots \\
    \sum^N_{n = 1}(g_1(\vb{x}_n) - y_n){x_n}_d = 0 \\
  \end{cases}
\end{align*}
Which leads to 
\begin{align*}
  \begin{cases}
    \sum^N_{n = 1}g_1(\vb{x}_n){x_n}_0 = \sum^N_{n = 1}y_n{x_n}_0 \\
    \sum^N_{n = 1}g_1(\vb{x}_n){x_n}_1 = \sum^N_{n = 1}y_n{x_n}_1 \\
    \vdots \\
    \sum^N_{n = 1}g_1(\vb{x}_n){x_n}_d = \sum^N_{n = 1}y_n{x_n}_d \\
  \end{cases}
\end{align*}
\newpage
By multiplying both sides of the $i$-th equation with $w_i$, where $\vb{w}$ is the weight vector of $g_1$, and summing all the resulting equations, we can get
\begin{align*}
  &\sum^d_{i = 0}\left(w_i\sum^N_{n = 1}g_1(\vb{x}_n){x_n}_i\right) = \sum^d_{i = 0}\left(w_i\sum^N_{n = 1}y_n{x_n}_i\right) \\ 
  \implies &\sum^N_{n = 1}\left(g_1(\vb{x}_n)\sum^d_{i = 0}w_i{x_n}_i\right) = \sum^N_{n = 1}\left(y_n\sum^d_{i = 0}w_i{x_n}_i\right) \\ 
  \implies &\sum^N_{n = 1}g_1(\vb{x}_n)\cdot g_1(\vb{x}_n) = \sum^N_{n = 1}y_ng_1(\vb{x}_n) \\ 
  \implies &\sum^N_{n = 1}g_1(\vb{x}_n)^2 = \sum^N_{n = 1}y_ng_1(\vb{x}_n) \\ 
\end{align*}
Lastly, it was previously derived that
\[
  \alpha_1 = \frac{\sum^N_{n = 1}g_1(\vb{x}_n)^2}{\sum^N_{n = 1}g_1(\vb{x}_n)y_n}
\]
Since 
\[
  \sum^N_{n = 1}g_1(\vb{x}_n)^2 = \sum^N_{n = 1}y_ng_1(\vb{x}_n)  
\]
we have proven 
\[
  \alpha_1 = 1
\]
Q.E.D.

To make the derivation more comprehensive, we use $s_{n, t}$ and $s_{n, t + 1}$ to denote $s_n$ before and after being updated in iteration $t$, repectively. \\ 
In iteration $t$, after obtaining the hypothesis $g_t = h$ using some regression algorithm on $\{\vb{x}_n, y_n - s_{n, t}\}$, $\alpha_t$ is found by 
\begin{align*}
  \alpha_t &= \arg\min_\eta\frac{1}{N}\sum^N_{n = 1}((y_n - s_{n, t}) - \eta g_t(\vb{x}_n))^2 \\ 
  &= \arg\min_\eta\sum^N_{n = 1}((y_n - s_{n, t}) - \eta g_t(\vb{x}_n))^2 \\ 
\end{align*}
The optimal $\eta$ can be found by solving 
\[
  \pdv{}{\eta}\left(\sum^N_{n = 1}((y_n - s_{n, t}) - \eta g_t(\vb{x}_n))^2\right) = -2\sum_{n = 1}^Ng_t(\vb{x}_n)(y_n - s_{n, t} - \eta g_t(\vb{x}_n)) = 0
\]
Hence we have
\begin{equation}
  \sum_{n = 1}^Ng_t(\vb{x}_n)(y_n - s_{n, t} - \alpha_t g_t(\vb{x}_n)) = 0
\end{equation}
\par 
After $\alpha_t$ and $g_t$ is found, $s_{n, t}$ are updated into
\[
  s_{n, t}\rightarrow s_{n, t + 1} = s_{n, t} + \alpha_tg_t(\vb{x}_n)
\]
Therefore, after the update, we have
\begin{align*}
  \sum^N_{n = 1}(y_n - s_{n, t + 1})g_t(\vb{x}_n) &= \sum^N_{n = 1}(y_n - (s_{n, t} + \alpha_tg_t(\vb{x}_n)))g_t(\vb{x}_n) \\ 
  &= \sum^N_{n = 1}(y_n - s_{n, t} - \alpha_tg_t(\vb{x}_n))g_t(\vb{x}_n) \\ 
  &= 0\quad(\text{by (4)})
\end{align*}
Hence we have proven that $\sum^N_{n = 1}(y_n - s_n)g_t(\vb{x}_n) = 0$ after updating all $s_n$ in iteration t using the steepest $\eta$ as $\alpha_t$. \\
Q.E.D.

The neural network with one hidden layer and $\tanh$ as the transformation function at each layer has the output of the form
\begin{align*}
  h(\vb{x}) &= \tanh(s_1^{(2)}) \\
  s_1^{(2)} &= \sum_{k = 1}^{d^{(1)}}w_{k, 1}^{(2)}x_k^{(1)}
\end{align*}
and 
\begin{align*}
  x_i^{(1)} &= \tanh(s_i^{(1)}) \\ 
  s_i^{(1)} &= \sum_{k = 1}^{d^{(0)}}w_{k, i}^{(1)}x_k^{(0)}
\end{align*}
With the error function
\[
  e_n = (y_n - h(\vb{x}_n))^2
\]
\par 
We then try to prove the following statement by induction
\begin{align}
  w_{i, j}^{(1)} &= w_{i, j + 1}^{(1)}\quad\text{for all $i$ and $1\leq j < d^{(1)}$} \\ 
  w_{i, 1}^{(2)} &= w_{i + 1, 1}^{(2)}\quad\text{for all $1\leq i < d^{(0)}$}
\end{align}
Suppose that after iteration $t$ the statement holds, then in iteration $t + 1$, we have
\begin{align*}
  \pdv{e_n}{w_{i, 1}^{(2)}} &= (\pdv{e_n}{s_1^{(2)}})(\pdv{s_1^{(2)}}{w_{i, 1}^{(2)}}) \\ 
  &= -2(y_n - \tanh(s_1^{(2)}))\tanh^\prime(s_1^{(2)})x_i^{(1)}
\end{align*}
and
\begin{align*}
  \pdv{e_n}{w_{i, j}^{(1)}} &= (\pdv{e_n}{s_j^{(1)}})(\pdv{s_j^{(1)}}{w_{i, j}^{(1)}}) \\ 
  &= (\pdv{e_n}{s_1^{(2)}})(\pdv{s_1^{(2)}}{s_j^{(1)}})x_i^{(0)} \\
  &= \left(-2(y_n - \tanh(s_1^{(2)}))\tanh^\prime(s_1^{(2)})\right)\left(w_{j, 1}^{(2)}\tanh^\prime(s_j^{(1)})\right)x_i^{(0)}
\end{align*}
\newpage
First we analyze $\pdv{e_n}{w_{i, 1}^{(2)}}$, we can see that the only term dependent of $i$ in its expression is $x_i^{(1)}$. \\ 
We also have
\begin{align*}
  x_i^{(1)} &= \tanh(s_i^{(1)}) \\ 
  &= \tanh(\sum_{k = 1}^{d^{(1)}}w_{k, i}^{(1)}x_k^{(0)}) \\
\end{align*}
With condition $(5)$, we know that $w_{k, i}^{(1)}$ is the same for all $1\leq i < d^{(1)}$, so $s_i^{(1)} = \sum_{k = 1}^{d^{(1)}}w_{k, i}^{(1)}x_k^{(0)}$ is independt of $i$. \\ 
Therefore, $x_i^{(1)}$ is also independent of $i$ and $\pdv{e_n}{w_{i, 1}^{(2)}}$ is independent of $i$. \\ 
In the $t + 1$-th iteration of the mini-batch gradient descent, $w_{i, 1}^{(2)}$ is updated proportional to the average of $\pdv{e_n}{w_{i, 1}^{(2)}}$ over some set of different $n$. Since we know that for any particular $n$, $\pdv{e_n}{w_{i, 1}^{(2)}}$ is the same for all $i$, after the update $w_{i, 1}^{(2)}$ is still the same for all $i$. \\ 
Hence condition $(6)$ still holds after the $t + 1$-th iteration. \\ 
\par 
Next, we analyze $\pdv{e_n}{w_{i, j}^{(1)}}$, the only term dependent of $j$ in its expression is $w_{j, 1}^{(2)}\tanh^\prime(s_j^{(1)})$ \\ 
By condition $(6)$, we know that $w_{j, 1}^{(2)}$ is the same for all $1\leq j\leq d^{(1)}$ \\ 
Also, in the previous part we have proven that $s_i^{(1)}$ is the same for all $1\leq i\leq d^{(1)}$ \\ 
Therefore, we can conclude that $\pdv{e_n}{w_{i, j}^{(1)}}$ is independent of $j$. \\ 
Similarly, in the $t + 1$-th iteration $w_{i, j}^{(1)}$ is updated proportional to the average of $\pdv{e_n}{w_{i, j}^{(1)}}$ over some different $n$. Since $\pdv{e_n}{w_{i, j}^{(1)}}$ is independent of $j$, $w_{i, j}^{(1)} = w_{i, j + 1}^{(1)}$ for all $i$ and $1\leq i < d^{(1)}$, and condition $(5)$ still holds after the $t+1$-th iteration. \\ 
\par
Lastly, at the beginning of the first iteration, all $w_{i, j}^{l}$ is set to the same value, 0.5, so the two conditions are satisfied. \\ 
Therefore by induction we've proven that $w_{i, j}^{(1)} = w_{i, j + 1}^{(1)}$ for all $i$ and $1\leq j < d^{(1)}$ ($(5)$) \\ 
Q.E.D.

Findings: \\
Form the plot we can see that both $U_t$ and $E_\text{in}$ decreases steadily throughout the iterations. The reason for the decrease in $E_\text{in}$ is described in P11. \\ 
For $U_t$, we have proven in problem 6 that $\frac{U_{t + 1}}{U_t} = 2\epsilon_t\sqrt{\epsilon_t(1 - \epsilon_t)}$, then, by AM-GM inequality, we have
\begin{align*}
  &\sqrt{\epsilon_t(1 - \epsilon_t)}\leq \frac{\epsilon_t + (1 - \epsilon_t)}{2} \\ 
  \implies &2\sqrt{\epsilon_t(1 - \epsilon_t)}\leq1
\end{align*}
Hence, $U_t$ must decrease monotonically, which fits our observation from the plot. \\ 
Also, from the plot we can see that as $t$ increases, both $U_t$ and $E_\text{in}$ decreases more slowly, and approaches some constant value asymptotically. This shows that the AdaBoost algorithm is indeed converging, and that after many number of iterations, the algorithm approaches a point where the parameters $\vb{u}$ stabilize, and $E_\text{in}$ can be improved no more.
